TURQUA // HOW 25 YEARS LAND ON ONE SCALE

Harmonization methodology & empirical validation from our NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026 presentation (MODIS C6.1 & VIIRS 375m, 2000–2026).

01 · BURNING DAYS

Vegetation-fire hotspots become 0.1° cell × local-solar-day events. One MODIS pixel or several VIIRS pixels: it burned.

LST = UTC + lon / 15 h | Activity frequency, not burned area

02 · ONE TARGET SATELLITE

Suomi-NPP's scale. Gaps are detected against twin NOAA-20, filled at measured twin ratio and flagged. NOAA-20/21 continue it after 2 Nov 2026.

Post-SNPP Bridge (2 Nov 2026 13:00 UTC): NOAA-20 (0.985) & NOAA-21 (0.978)

03 · TWO-BRANCH TRANSFER

Learned where MODIS and VIIRS overlap, per land cover × calendar month (s). Fires MODIS missed are placed from MODIS activity nearby.

MODIS saw: V ≈ k₁[s] · M_cell | Unseen: V ≈ k₂[s] · M_neighbourhood

04 · RECONSTRUCT + INTERVAL

2001–Jan 2012 rebuilt from MODIS, hatched in the calendar, with an uncertainty interval taken from held-out-year errors (80% CI: [q₁₀, q₉₀]).

TURQUA FIRE HARMONY / METHODOLOGY & SCIENTIFIC ATTRIBUTION

MISSION AND PURPOSE

This workspace harmonizes MODIS and VIIRS active hotspot records across user-defined areas of interest and timeframes. The calendar provides scientific context to evaluate long-term fire regimes without sensor discontinuities. Per NASA FIRMS documentation, each detection represents the center of a pixel that contains one or more active fires or thermal anomalies, rather than the exact fire boundary.

SATELLITE CONTINUITY & HARMONIZATION

MODIS and VIIRS are distinct sensors with nominal footprints of ~1 km (MODIS) and ~375 m (VIIRS). When newer satellites joined orbit (2012 Suomi-NPP, 2018/2020 NOAA-20, 2024 NOAA-21), raw hotspot counts increased artificially due to higher spatial resolution and frequent revisits. TURQUA harmonizes multi-satellite detections onto a common 0.1° Burning Day grid with a measured two-branch transfer ratio k₁ = 2.9705, restoring observational continuity across sensor generations.

NASA GIBS SATELLITE IMAGERY

Imagery layers are provided by NASA GIBS (Global Imagery Browse Services), part of the Earth Science Data and Information System (ESDIS). True Color, false-color thermal (M11-I2-I1), and MODIS MCD12Q1 annual land cover products provide visual and environmental context.

STATIC THERMAL ANOMALIES (STA)

Permanent industrial flares, steel mills, and port operations are filtered using known geospatial boundaries (e.g. Payas/Iskenderun, Aliaga, Dilovasi) to prevent industrial heat signatures from contaminating wildland fire regimes.

DATA PRIVACY & SECURITY

This application runs entirely client-side. No user queries, API keys, or session identifiers are transmitted to external servers. Preloaded datasets run completely offline.
