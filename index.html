TURQUA FIRE MAP ALARM / METHODOLOGY & SCIENTIFIC ATTRIBUTION

MISSION AND PURPOSE

This workspace harmonizes MODIS and VIIRS active hotspot records across user-defined areas of interest and timeframes. The calendar provides scientific context to evaluate long-term fire regimes without sensor discontinuities. Per NASA FIRMS documentation, each detection represents the center of a pixel that contains one or more active fires or thermal anomalies, rather than the exact fire boundary.

SATELLITE CONTINUITY & HARMONIZATION

MODIS and VIIRS are distinct sensors with nominal footprints of ~1 km (MODIS) and ~375 m (VIIRS). When newer satellites joined orbit (2012 Suomi-NPP, 2018/2020 NOAA-20, 2024 NOAA-21), raw hotspot counts increased artificially by ×17.49 due to increased observation frequency and finer spatial resolution. TURQUA harmonizes detections onto a common 0.1° Burning Day scale, stabilizing the cross-era ratio to ×1.13 and removing 98% of artificial sensor inflation (Leave-One-Year-Out validation r = 0.989).

NASA GIBS SATELLITE IMAGERY

Imagery layers are provided by NASA GIBS (Global Imagery Browse Services), part of the Earth Science Data and Information System (ESDIS). True Color, false-color thermal (M11-I2-I1), and MODIS MCD12Q1 annual land cover products provide visual and environmental context.

STATIC THERMAL ANOMALIES (STA)

Permanent industrial flares, steel mills, and port operations are filtered using known geospatial boundaries (e.g. Payas/İskenderun, Aliağa, Dilovası) to prevent industrial heat signatures from contaminating wildland fire regimes.

DATA PRIVACY & SECURITY

This application runs entirely client-side. No user queries, API keys, or session identifiers are transmitted to external servers. Preloaded datasets run completely offline.
